Prepare the property information

Gather the property location, whether this is a new build or replacement, the intended building use, and any existing system records. Do not upload deeds, identity documents, or other sensitive files through this referral form.

Confirm the permitting authority

Requirements depend on the property and the applicable authority. Start with TCEQ’s on-site sewage facility resources and confirm the local process before authorizing work. A mailing town alone does not establish jurisdiction.

Liberty County: what a new system application needs

For property under the Liberty County order, the county's Building and Septic Permit Guide (updated August 2025) says the application must include:

  • A site evaluation
  • A design drawing by a registered sanitarian or engineer
  • A signed two year maintenance agreement
  • An affidavit to the public recorded at the County Clerk's office

The county lists its Permit and Inspection office phone as (936) 336-4560. Confirm current requirements and fees with that office before you sign a proposal.

Lot size and sewer rules that can stop a project

The county order sets lot sizes for single family subdivisions platted after June 1, 1986: at least half an acre with public water, or one acre with a private water well. It also says a new septic system is not allowed within 300 feet of an existing public sewer system unless that system denies service in writing. Ask the installer to confirm both points for your lot before design work starts.

Adding a building to a lot with an existing system

The permit guide says a building permit is required for any building 100 square feet or larger, and that the county will not issue it until you show an adequate septic system exists or buy a permit for a new one. Proof is a Liberty County Septic Verification Report from a Texas licensed installer or engineer. Crosby and Huffman are in Harris County, where these Liberty County rules do not apply. More detail is in our Liberty County septic rules guide.

Compare scope, not just the headline price

Ask whether evaluation, design, permits, equipment, installation, electrical work, restoration, and ongoing maintenance are included. Ask who is responsible for each item and what remains outside the proposal.
