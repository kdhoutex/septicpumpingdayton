Start with the existing service record

Check your system paperwork for the model, recent service visits, and the maintenance provider’s contact information. If you already have a maintenance arrangement, ask that provider what is covered before paying for a separate visit.

Give useful observations, not a diagnosis

Note when the problem began, which alarm or indicator is showing, and any recent service or power interruption. You can report what you observe without deciding whether an aerator, float, pump, or controller has failed.

Check the right credentials

Texas has distinct credentials for aerobic maintenance and installation work. Ask who will perform the work and verify the credential relevant to that scope using TCEQ’s occupational licensing information. A pumping business and an aerobic maintenance provider are not interchangeable roles.

Before authorizing repair
