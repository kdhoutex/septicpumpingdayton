LIBERTY COUNTY, TEXAS

Septic service in Liberty County, TX: the local rules to know first

Liberty County runs its own septic permit program under a county order approved by the state in 2023. Some of its rules are stricter than the Texas minimum, and they change who you should call first.

Who handles septic systems in Liberty County

The Liberty County Permit and Inspection Department handles on-site sewage facility (OSSF) permits, the official name for septic systems. The county's current order was adopted by the Commissioners Court on October 24, 2023 and approved by the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) on December 1, 2023. The county lists its office phone as (936) 336-4560 on its permits and licenses page.

The order covers Liberty County except areas regulated under another order, ordinance, or resolution. If your home is inside a city, ask the city or the county which office handles your system. Crosby and Huffman are in Harris County, so the Liberty County rules on this page do not apply there.

Aerobic systems: a maintenance contract for the life of the system

Statewide, TCEQ says that two years after an aerobic treatment unit is installed, homeowners may maintain it themselves, and that some permitting authorities have stricter rules (TCEQ maintenance page). Liberty County is one of them. Its order says homeowners must keep in force a maintenance agreement with a licensed maintenance company for the life of the system.

  • For homes, the county order sets maintenance at 4 visits per year.
  • Maintenance reports are sent to the Liberty County Permit Clerk by email.
  • The contract must show the permit number, the maintenance provider's license, start and end dates, and the provider's phone and emergency phone.
  • The county may inspect a system that uses aerobic treatment at any time.

What this means for you: if an aerobic alarm sounds, start with the provider named on your contract, and check the end date on that contract. The county posts one year and two year maintenance contract forms on its permits page.

A maintenance visit is not a pump-out

A maintenance provider inspects the system's parts and reports on them. Pumping the tank is a separate job. TCEQ recommends pumping a conventional septic tank every three to five years, and says aerobic tanks are pumped based on sludge depth and the manufacturer's recommendations. Ask your maintenance provider for the sludge reading from the last report before you book pumping.

Companies that haul septage are registered with TCEQ as sludge transporters, and TCEQ links to a database of them from its maintenance page. Ask a pumping company for its registration before work starts.

Adding a building or installing a new system

The county's Building and Septic Permit Guide (updated August 2025) says a building permit is required for any building 100 square feet or larger, and that the county will not issue it until you prove an adequate septic system exists or buy a permit for a new one. Proof is a Liberty County Septic Verification Report from a Texas licensed installer or engineer. That report records the system type, tank, sludge level, the condition of the inlet and outlet tees, whether the aerator, irrigation pump, and disinfection device work, and any sign of trouble in the disposal area.

For a new system, the guide says the application must include a site evaluation, a design drawing by a registered sanitarian or engineer, a signed two year maintenance agreement, and an affidavit to the public recorded at the County Clerk's office. The guide lists the septic permit fee as $275, and the county order adds a $10 state fee. Confirm current fees with the county before you apply.

The county order also sets lot sizes for single family subdivisions platted after June 1, 1986: at least half an acre with public water, or one acre with a private water well. A new septic system is not allowed within 300 feet of an existing public sewer system unless that system denies service in writing.

Questions to ask any Liberty County septic provider

  • What is your TCEQ license or registration for this job? Installer, maintenance provider, and sludge transporter are different roles. You can check credentials through TCEQ's OSSF licensing page.
  • For aerobic service: will you be the provider named on my county maintenance contract, and will you send the reports to the county?
  • For pumping: which tanks are included, and will you note the sludge level and the condition of the tees?
  • What is included in the price, and will I get a written receipt?
